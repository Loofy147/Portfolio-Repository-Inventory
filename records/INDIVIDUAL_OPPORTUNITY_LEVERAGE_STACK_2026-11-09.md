# Individual Opportunity Leverage Stack — Operating Model v0.1

**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**Applicant/operating unit:** Individual resident in Algeria; no incorporated startup assumed.  
**Purpose:** combine small, independently accessible opportunities into a larger capability, cash-flow, proof and distribution base without making startup formation the prerequisite.

**Consolidated research record:** [Individual-First Opportunity Discovery Dossier](INDIVIDUAL_FIRST_OPPORTUNITY_DISCOVERY_DOSSIER_2026-11-09.md).

## 1. Strategic correction

The target is not to become a startup merely to qualify for startup programmes. The operating unit is currently an individual. That is a valid applicant/delivery form for a meaningful set of grants, fellowships, open-source funds, research programmes, bounties and service contracts.

"Small opportunity" describes the entry size, not the ambition or ultimate scale. A series of bounded, low-capital opportunities can be combined to fund and prove a much larger technical or commercial capability.

The goal is to compound:
- **cash:** paid tasks, bounties, individual awards, stipends, milestone grants, prizes and service exports;
- **resource access:** model/API allowances, compute, hosting, storage, datasets, technical tools, laboratories and research facilities;
- **proof:** accepted deliverables, merged contributions, reproducible results, public artifacts, evaluation records and references;
- **distribution/access:** maintainers, users, technical communities, specialist buyers, research networks, provider ecosystems and subcontract channels;
- **capability:** reusable components, methods, domain knowledge, credentials, legal/payment rails and delivery routines.

Do not count all five as cash. Record each value type separately, and combine only when its contribution is measurable.

## 2. Opportunity stacking architecture

### Layer A — individual cash opportunities

Search continuously for:
- individual fellowships and independent researcher grants;
- open-source maintainer/contributor grants;
- bug bounties, vulnerability disclosure rewards and security contests;
- paid technical issues, code review, testing, documentation, accessibility and implementation tasks;
- short specialist subcontracting packages;
- evaluation, benchmark, reproducibility and research replication work;
- public technical challenges, prizes and competitions;
- paid data preparation, tooling, migration, quality review and integration work;
- project milestones funded by foundations or public-interest technology programmes.

A task is only useful if actual scope, acceptance, payout method, country eligibility, KYC, time-to-payment, tax and delivery liability are understood.

### Layer B — non-cash capability resources

Search for:
- free or subsidised model APIs;
- individual developer and research compute;
- shared GPUs, notebooks and accelerator hours;
- object storage, edge functions, deployment and database quotas;
- security, accessibility and privacy audits;
- paid-for testing, mentoring, research access, equipment and datasets;
- open standards, public procurement datasets and public technical documentation.

Value an allowance only against a real workload that would otherwise require paid spend. A headline credit balance with no eligible workload has near-zero realised value.

### Layer C — proof and reputation assets

Prioritise opportunities whose accepted outputs can be reused:
- public, reproducible code and tests;
- upstream pull requests and maintained packages;
- security findings accepted under a programme's rules;
- independent evaluations and benchmark receipts;
- datasets with documented provenance and lawful licences;
- technical reports that buyers, maintainers or research communities can inspect;
- public demos with actual users and measured limitations.

A grant or small paid task that creates a referenceable artifact can improve access to larger future work. This option value is recorded separately from immediate money.

### Layer D — distribution and access

Potential compounding routes include:
- open-source project ecosystems and maintainers;
- research foundations and technical communities;
- hackathons and challenge organisers;
- procurement/subcontracting programmes;
- provider marketplaces and referral programmes;
- user groups, industry associations and specialist buyers;
- public-interest technology networks and fiscal hosts where legally appropriate.

A network or event is a channel, not revenue. Count actual buyer meetings, accepted work, access granted or distribution, not attendance or impressions.

### Layer E — reusable capability capital

Combine compatible opportunities to build a larger base:
- cash from a paid task funds the next task's testing or data costs;
- compute credits reduce experimentation cost;
- a public contribution proves execution capability;
- a grant funds a bounded open technology asset;
- that asset creates a reusable method or library;
- the method reduces delivery cost on a later paid engagement;
- delivery evidence creates references and access to larger assignments.

Do not create one large interdependent proposal by stitching together unrelated grants. Each funder must receive a distinct, honest scope and each workstream must remain independently deliverable under its terms.

## 5. Algeria payment and legal architecture

Do not treat every incoming foreign payment as the same transaction. Separate at least these types:

1. **Grant/fellowship/stipend:** research or public-interest funding. Its legal/tax/bank classification is distinct from payment for exported services.
2. **Prize/bounty/reward:** payment for a competition result or a programme-defined security contribution. Check contest and payout terms, IP transfer, tax and KYC.
3. **Service-export income:** a foreign customer pays for software, research, testing, design, documentation or consulting delivered from Algeria.
4. **Resource credit:** provider credit or free-tier access; no cash is received, and provider terms govern use.

### Official route worth validating for individual service exports

Bank of Algeria Instruction 06-2021 establishes the foreign-currency merchant account and **professional non-merchant foreign-currency account**. Article 2 authorises a resident natural or legal person carrying on lawful economic activity to open such account(s). Article 4 allows the full amount of export receipts from goods/services to be credited when repatriated within regulatory deadlines. Article 12 ties use of the professional non-merchant account to activity-related expenses and electronic payments.

This creates a possible professional route that does not inherently require forming a startup company. It is not a blanket right to route arbitrary personal remittances into a professional account: the activity must be lawful, the account must be correctly classified, the bank's supporting-document and declaration requirements must be met, and the current foreign-exchange rules must be applied. Regulation 26-02 of 23 July 2026 later amended Regulation 07-01; obtain the current consolidated rule and written bank instructions before contracting. Grant/stipend receipt must be separately classified; do not invoice a grant as a fictitious service export.

### Optional ANAE route

ANAE's official portal lists eligibility conditions and activities such as digital services, consulting/expertise/training and business services. Auto-entrepreneur status may be a practical individual activity/invoicing wrapper for an eligible activity. It is not a startup label and should not be adopted automatically: verify the exact activity code, turnover ceiling, invoicing, tax and payment conditions. It may be inappropriate if expected turnover or activity falls outside its limits.

### Lawful constraint-solving principles

When a government or provider constraint blocks a route:
- identify the exact transaction being constrained (grant, service export, import, data transfer, payment, licensed activity);
- identify the exact legal actor/role and required registration or approval;
- select a lawful individual, non-trader professional, auto-entrepreneur, host/fiscal sponsor, subcontractor or licensed-partner structure where the rules permit;
- choose interoperable/open architecture, local execution, data minimisation, self-hosting or a different provider if the constraint is about technology/location/data;
- document why the alternative is lawful and what obligations remain.

Never bypass by false residence, borrowed accounts, hidden beneficial ownership, misdescription of funds, evading KYC/sanctions, or treating a restricted activity as an unregulated one.

## 6. Compounding model

Track each opportunity using three separate value columns:

### Cash value
Actual amount payable, currency, timing, fees/tax, acceptance requirements and probability. Never convert an advertised maximum into expected cash.

### Avoided cash cost
The amount of real, planned expenditure replaced by a free allowance. Only count the actual workload's replacement cost; exclude unused quotas and inflated retail prices.

### Capability and access value
Reusable tools, proof, reputation, technical access, dataset rights, maintainer relationships or distribution. Record separately; do not combine it with cash as though it were liquid.

A small opportunity advances the stack when it does at least one of these:
- produces positive net cash with short time-to-payment;
- buys compute or infrastructure that would otherwise be paid for;
- produces a reusable public artifact;
- creates a verified track record or accepted reference;
- opens access to a buyer, funder, dataset, platform or technical community;
- reduces a future delivery's cost, risk or time.

A candidate is rejected when its application effort, taxes, legal cost, working capital, payment delay, IP restrictions, privacy obligations or switching cost exceed the realistic value.

### Lane 3 — individual paid work

Build a recurring screen for:
- security bounties and audit challenges;
- short issue/fix/testing/documentation contracts;
- paid data/research/evaluation tasks;
- provider-to-provider subcontracting;
- individual technical contests and prizes.

For every programme, validate Algeria payout and legal restrictions before producing work. Keep a no-pay/no-cashflow watch status when payout is unresolved.

### Lane 4 — payment and legal readiness

Ask the bank in writing:
- requirements for a professional non-merchant foreign-currency account for a resident individual;
- acceptable professional status and activity evidence;
- required pre-declaration or project documentation for digital service exports;
- how grant/fellowship payments, prizes and bounties are classified separately from service exports;
- supporting records, foreign exchange retention/repatriation, fees and timeline under current Regulation 26-02 and Instruction 06-2021.

No registration, account or tax status is changed by this research record.
