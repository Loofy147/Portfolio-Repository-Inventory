# Algeria Economic Reality Audit — 2026-09-23

Status: OPEN / evidence layer

## Purpose

Re-check the market-entry assumptions against current observable purchasing and outsourcing behavior in Algeria.

## Established / strong market signals

### 1. External B2B service purchasing exists

Current 2026 procurement evidence from Optimum Telecom Algérie (Djezzy) shows repeated purchasing of external services:
- technical assistance for site realization, ultimately awarded to SARL BAVELY TELECOM for four lots;
- employee satisfaction survey by a specialized cabinet;
- ISO 9001 certification-accompaniment service;
- customs brokerage;
- packaging/conditioning of SIM cards;
- other technical, IT and facility services.

Sources:
Djezzy procurement and award notices, including 11/2026/OTA/DIR and related 2026 notices.

Interpretation:
The direct-payment structure "company → service provider → bounded service → acceptance" is real.

Limit:
Large-company procurement often imposes references, revenue thresholds, registration and formal prequalification. This is an entry barrier, not proof of a small-entry wedge.

### 2. Specialist capacity is externally supplied

Current Algerian market signals include multiple 2026 offers from independent engineers for:
- civil engineering, quantities, contract variations, payment situations and technical/financial bids;
- mechanical CAD and simulation;
- electrical engineering studies;
- BIM/quantity/cost work;
- industrial maintenance consulting.

Several offers explicitly target companies, engineering offices and contractors and describe freelance/subcontracting delivery.

Interpretation:
A provider-to-provider purchasing path exists as a market structure:
**established provider/BET/company → specialist subcontractor/freelancer → deliverable**.

Limit:
Marketplace advertisements show supply and market intent, not completed paid transactions. Do not upgrade them to paid-demand evidence.

### 3. Industrial subcontracting is an explicit policy and market direction

AAPI publicly emphasized development of qualified national subcontractors providing components, spare parts and industrial services. The 2026 IDMAJ initiative explicitly targets linking large order-givers with Algerian SMEs and highlights industrial subcontracting, quality/compliance and Industry 4.0.

Interpretation:
The ecosystem is actively trying to increase the number of small suppliers around larger enterprises.

Limit:
Institutional promotion is not proof that a specific new entrant will receive an order.

### 4. Specialist/temporary external capacity is more visible than routine-function outsourcing

Evidence reviewed:
- routine procurement, stock, billing/collection, maintenance and HSE roles are commonly staffed internally;
- specialist external offers are common for engineering, certification support, cyber, and project-specific technical work;
- CERIST solicited a 12-month managed cybersecurity service including audit, continuous monitoring and incident response;
- Djezzy procured technical assistance and ISO accompaniment.

Inference:
Externalization is more plausible when at least one of these is true:
1. scarce expertise;
2. temporary/peaky workload;
3. independent specialist output is useful;
4. external equipment/capability is cheaper than owning it;
5. certification/accreditation or specialist methodology is required;
6. project-specific capacity is needed.

Status: INFERENCE, supported by observed cases but not a universal rule.

### 5. Cash collection is a material economic constraint

ONS Q1 2025 industrial survey reports that lengthening receivables collection periods, high operating costs and debt repayments continue to affect treasury conditions; more than 22% of private-sector respondents reported using bank credit without difficulty.

Interpretation:
A low-capital entrant should strongly prefer short time-to-cash and avoid business models where customer payment depends on a long public/insurance/downstream collection chain.

Source:
ONS, Enquête sur la situation et les perspectives dans l'industrie, 1T 2025.

### 6. Industrial productivity problems remain real

The same ONS industrial survey reports equipment breakdown/obsolescence and maintenance issues among responding firms and indicates that many respondents see equipment renewal as a route to increased production without adding staff.

Interpretation:
Operational-loss detection remains a valid economic-reality domain.

Limit:
The survey does not identify an external buyer for a new diagnostic service. Payer remains UNKNOWN.

## Important negative findings

### Large procurement is not a low-capital entry path by default

Djezzy's 2026 technical-assistance procurement required average annual revenue of at least 60 million DZD for the supplier. Other Djezzy tenders require prior references and minimum revenue levels.

Therefore:
Do not mistake "real paid market" for "reachable first customer."

### Regulated consulting can be bought, but entry requirements matter

Examples:
- Djezzy procured ISO 9001 accompaniment and required two comparable projects for suppliers.
- Halliburton's HESP previously procured training/accompaniment for Law 18-07 compliance.
- BAOSEM published a diagnostic of hydrocarbon installations under Decree 21-331, but the notice was later marked unsuccessful.

Interpretation:
Regulatory/compliance demand can be commercialized, but reference/credential barriers and existing specialists are real.

### Marketplace pricing is not payment evidence

Ouedkniss offers with displayed prices or negotiable prices are signals of supply and packaging, not proof that the quoted price is regularly paid.

## New market-entry hypothesis

**Provider-to-provider wedge**

A low-capital entrant may have an easier first transaction by selling a narrow execution capability to an existing provider that already owns:
- customer relationship;
- trust;
- legal/technical role;
- procurement channel;
- domain context.

Example structure:
**Established provider → PO/subcontract → narrow production task → provider acceptance → direct payment.**

Candidate capabilities need not be "software". They may be:
- structured document extraction;
- technical consistency checking;
- evidence-pack assembly;
- data reconciliation;
- report generation;
- research/verification;
- back-office technical production.

Status: HYPOTHESIS / OPEN.

## Required kill test for provider-to-provider wedge

Kill if:
- provider can perform the task cheaply in-house;
- task is too generic/commodity-priced;
- provider requires certifications we cannot supply;
- provider will not expose sufficient data;
- payment terms are materially longer than direct SME work;
- the provider captures most value while price to us becomes too low;
- subcontracting contract creates unacceptable liability/IP/customer-confidentiality risk.

## Current economic reality map

The market presently supports several distinct payment shapes:

1. Large-company formal procurement → supplier.
2. Private company → specialist professional service.
3. Engineering/BET/company → freelance specialist/subcontractor.
4. Company → ongoing managed service.
5. Company → project-specific capacity.
6. Buyer → physical subcontracted industrial production.

The research question is now:

**Which of these payment shapes can accommodate a first transaction that is technically useful, legally simple, low-capital, fast to deliver, and accessible without an established reputation?**

## Next discriminating search

Search provider-side purchasing rather than end-user problems.

Target:
- engineering offices;
- certification/consulting firms;
- industrial maintenance firms;
- integrators;
- software/IT firms;
- logistics/compliance providers;
- laboratories/technical service firms.

For each:
- what do they routinely subcontract?
- what deliverables are handed to subcontractors?
- what is paid per task?
- what can be completed remotely?
- what requires no regulated signature?
- what has a short acceptance cycle?
- who signs the subcontract/PO?
- what evidence is required to onboard a new subcontractor?

Do not infer paid demand from job listings or marketplace offers. Seek actual tenders, awarded contracts, explicit subcontracting terms, or direct buyer evidence.
