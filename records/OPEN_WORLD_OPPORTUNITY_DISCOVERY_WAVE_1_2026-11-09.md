# Open-World Economic Opportunity Discovery — Wave 1

**Research date:** 2026-11-09  
**Search type:** Open-world, economic-problem-led, multi-sector and multi-geography scan  
**Status:** Screening result — no entry candidate has passed every hard gate  
**Search boundary:** No project or repository used as the starting filter. Existing portfolio assets were not used to rank candidates.

## Executive decision

This wave produced three research-worthy opportunity frontiers, two useful enablers/channels, and several explicit pre-kills.

**No candidate is promoted to ENTRY-CANDIDATE or PAID-TEST-READY.** The evidence establishes some real market and regulatory pressure, but does not yet establish a named accessible buyer, demonstrated willingness to pay, or an accepted paid intervention that this entrant can deliver with current legal/technical authority and manageable risk.

### Highest-priority research frontier

**Installation-level CBAM data preparation for Algerian cement/clinker exporters to the EU.**

- EU trade data sourced through World Bank WITS/UN Comtrade reports EU imports of **1.449 million tonnes** of Algerian cement clinker in 2025, valued at about **US$92.2 million**.
- The EU CBAM definitive regime applies from 1 January 2026 and includes cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen.
- Non-EU installation operators must monitor and calculate embedded emissions and provide information to accredited verifiers. Actual emissions verification is a regulated/accredited function. A bounded data-preparation service could be investigated, but must not claim to certify or verify emissions.
- The remaining hard blockers are buyer access, evidence that the exporter/importer has an unmet data-preparation gap, technical expertise, procurement route, price, and actual data access.

**Do not build CBAM software or sell generic compliance consulting.** Existing software, advisory firms and verifier groups already serve the category. The only plausible wedge worth testing is specific installation data readiness and evidence assembly under an independent accredited verifier's workflow.

## 1. Search scope and method

This wave sampled the following failure/economic patterns across several markets:

- regulatory cost and data obligations at the export/import boundary;
- supplier evidence and product-data gaps in changing product rules;
- industrial energy consumption and production efficiency;
- subcontracting, buyer access and procurement channels;
- SME financing and expansion constraints;
- late payment and working-capital stress;
- vending/onsite service procurement and incumbent density;
- incumbent software, verification, audit and service alternatives.

Discovery and pre-kill were run together. A national statistic, legal deadline, public listing, grant or vendor absence was not treated as proof of a purchasable unmet need.

This is a focused wave, not a census of all global markets. It did not conduct direct customer interviews, supplier/buyer outreach, site visits, live tender-document retrieval, or paid tests. No counterparty was contacted.

### OW-01 — CBAM data preparation: evidence and exact boundary

**Established**
- EU CBAM definitive regime applies from **1 January 2026**.
- Covered sectors include cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen.
- World Bank WITS, using UN Comtrade trade data, reports that the EU imported **1,448,890,000 kg** of cement clinker (HS 252310) from Algeria in 2025, with a reported trade value of **US$92,203.39 thousand**.
- Non-EU installation operators monitor and calculate embedded emissions under CBAM methodology and provide data/evidence to accredited verifiers.
- Actual emissions data must be verified by independent verifiers accredited by an EU/EEA national accreditation body. Companies established outside the EU may apply to an EU national accreditation body that offers CBAM accreditation.

**Not established**
- That all 2025 trade volume remains at the same level in 2026.
- That a specific Algerian installation is using default values rather than actual emissions.
- That the default-value outcome is more expensive than actual verified emissions for a particular installation/product.
- That any particular exporter lacks a capable internal team or existing adviser.
- That a buyer will pay a new entrant for data-preparation work.
- That a non-accredited party may represent its work as verification or certification. It may not.

**Bounded intervention hypothesis**
An installation-specific data-readiness service: map the required emissions fields to source documents, energy/fuel/material/production records, reconcile data quality and missing evidence, prepare an auditable handoff pack for the operator's chosen accredited verifier, and track remediation items. The deliverable is a preparation pack and data-gap register, not an assurance opinion, verification report, regulatory declaration or guaranteed acceptance.

**Potential buyer/payer**
The installation operator/exporter, or possibly the EU importer that bears CBAM declaration/certificate obligations. Procurement authority and budget owner must be named in discovery, not assumed.

**Acceptance test**
A named operator and independent accredited verifier confirm that the agreed data pack is complete enough for the verifier's review workflow, with all exceptions and limitations recorded. This is not a guarantee that emissions values will be accepted or produce a lower certificate cost.

**Economics / risk**
Revenue, fee, cost to serve, on-site measurement cost, travel, expert review, liability, procurement timeline and time-to-cash are **UNKNOWN**. A fixed-scope desk-based gap assessment may reduce capital, but the core technical work still requires qualified industrial emissions expertise and source access. No savings claim can be made without a specific installation's actual and default-value comparison.

**Next discriminating test**
Identify one exporter/installation with verified EU-bound CBAM goods, one responsible buyer and its existing verifier/adviser. Ask whether it already has an installation monitoring plan, validated data, verifier contract and data collection process. Test whether there is an unowned, recurring and paid gap. Stop if the work is fully internalised or already delivered by the verifier/adviser.

### OW-02 — Battery Passport: regulatory need, but a poor generic product wedge

**Established**
- Article 77 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 applies from **18 February 2027** to each LMT battery, each industrial battery above 2 kWh and each EV battery placed on the EU market or put into service.
- The economic operator placing the battery on the market is responsible for accuracy, completeness and updates; it may authorise another operator in writing to create or update the passport.
- The EU DPP Registry became operational on 20 July 2026; the Commission says certain battery passports become mandatory in February 2027.
- A dense vendor landscape already exists, including Spherity, Minespider, Circularise, Narravero, Siemens, osapiens, Batteriepasswerk and others. Several offer pilots or public low/mid-market subscriptions.

**Decision**
Do not build a generic battery-passport platform or QR-code application. A possible service role would be product data onboarding, supplier evidence mapping or authorised passport operations for a specific underserved manufacturer/importer. No such buyer has been identified in this wave.

**Kill criteria**
- No target battery product is actually in Article 77 scope.
- The operator already has a platform/service provider that can onboard the product.
- Data rights/access cannot be obtained from suppliers.
- The responsible operator will not grant written authorisation or accept a clear responsibility boundary.
- The only value is generating QR codes or basic product pages already offered cheaply by vendors.

### OW-03 — Industrial energy-efficiency implementation: retain only a qualified-partner path

**Established**
- APRUE's LC-Industry programme describes non-repayable financial assistance for feasibility studies and investment projects to improve industrial energy efficiency, with support calculated conditionally based on the study/operation cost. Public and private industrial establishments under Algerian law are described as target beneficiaries.
- APRUE states that mandatory periodic energy audits apply at thresholds of 2,000 toe/year for industry, 1,000 toe/year for transport and 500 toe/year for tertiary establishments.
- APRUE maintains an approved-auditor list (updated as of March 2026) and describes technical qualifications/training for industrial energy auditors.

**Pre-kill**
- Generic statutory energy-audit service: **KILLED for unapproved entry**. Do not claim approved-auditor status or provide a legally recognised audit without the required credentials.
- Generic energy dashboard or energy consultancy: already a visible category; no specific unmet paid gap demonstrated.
- One-off hardware sale without an accepted baseline/savings calculation: insufficient evidence.

**Surviving hypothesis**
A bounded measurement/data-collection or implementation package for a specific energy loss, delivered as a subcontractor to an approved auditor/ESCO or equipment supplier, and linked to an eligible feasibility/investment project. The facility pays for an accepted deliverable or the equipment/implementation; the APRUE scheme may reduce project cost but does not guarantee payment to an unqualified service provider.

**Next discriminating test**
Find one plant with a documented recurring energy loss, one approved auditor or qualified ESCO, and one APRUE-supported intervention. Establish the budget owner, eligible scope, required accreditation, project contribution, acceptance metric and payment cycle before quoting.

## 8. Next discriminating tests

### Test 1 — CBAM: identify one specific installation and its existing process

**Required evidence**
- One named Algerian installation exporting a CBAM good to an EU buyer.
- Exact CN code and EU destination confirmation.
- Existing emissions-monitoring method, internal owner, external adviser/verifier, data gaps and whether actual emissions or default values are used.
- A named budget owner/payer and purchasing route.
- A specialist capable of performing the data-preparation work without claiming verification authority.

**Continue only if**
- a recurring data/evidence gap is confirmed;
- the plant/importer is willing to pay for a bounded, accepted deliverable;
- an independent accredited verifier is comfortable with the separation of roles;
- technical expertise and access are secured.

**Stop if** the work is already internalised or contracted, the value of actual data cannot justify the service, the responsible buyer cannot be reached, or the entrant cannot obtain the required technical competence and access.

### Test 2 — ALGEST: use the event to discover a buyer, not to promote a predetermined product

Before attending, shortlist target industries and ask for the professional visitor conditions. Do not purchase the €714 B2B package unless the organiser can confirm multiple named meetings with relevant buyers.

At the event, test repeated economic problems such as:
- equipment downtime and long spare-part lead times;
- repeated rejection/rework due to specification mismatch;
- supplier qualification or subcontractor acceptance;
- delayed technical/quality documentation;
- excess energy consumption with a measurable production consequence;
- commissioning and acceptance evidence gaps.

Capture the specific event, loss owner, current workaround, current provider, budget owner, actual payer and whether a fixed deliverable can be bought. Do not sell a generic software dashboard.

### Test 3 — Energy efficiency: one plant + one approved auditor/ESCO

Confirm programme terms in writing with APRUE, including current funds, eligible project/study costs, applicant criteria, contribution, contracting/payment process and whether an external service subcontractor is eligible. Then select one plant with an observable loss and a qualified partner. Do not perform or advertise the statutory audit unless approved.

### Test 4 — Battery Passport: prove a customer segment before any build

Find one battery manufacturer/importer that places an in-scope battery on the EU market and has not already bought a solution. Compare current platform onboarding, supplier-data collection and authorisation responsibilities. Offer no software until a named buyer identifies a gap that an existing platform/support package does not cover.

## 9. Decision

**Overall result: NO-GO for a new product build or capital commitment in this wave. RESEARCH-GO for two narrowly bounded investigations: (1) CBAM installation data readiness and (2) buyer discovery at ALGEST.**

The strongest market evidence currently belongs to CBAM-related Algerian clinker exports into the EU. However, the payer, unmet data gap, actual-versus-default cost difference, expertise requirement and accepted paid deliverable are still unresolved. This is not yet an executable business.

The best low-cost immediate move is buyer discovery through the forthcoming industrial/subcontracting channel, provided professional visitor access is affordable. If no specific buyer/problem/payable outcome is discovered, the correct conclusion is no-go—not a new dashboard, marketplace, or generic advisory business.

No application, purchase, registration, customer contact, contract or account change was made.

### OW-07 — Methane measurement/data services in the Algeria–EU gas supply chain

**ESTABLISHED**
- Eurostat reports that Algeria accounted for **17.4%** of EU natural-gas imports in gaseous state in 2025; WITS/UN Comtrade records EU imports from Algeria at a reported USD 11.064bn and 20.277bn kg under HS 271121. Use Eurostat's share for the comparison; trade-data mass/value are not methane emissions.
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 creates producer/importer methane measurement, monitoring, reporting and verification requirements.
- The Commission's March 2026 importer Q&A states the information importers need, the equivalent producer-level monitoring standard and independent third-party verification requirements.
- **Regulatory timing changed materially:** Parliament voted on amendments on 22 October 2026 and referred the file back for interinstitutional negotiation; the Council agreed its negotiating position on 6 November 2026. The Council proposes postponing importer MRV reporting from 1 January 2027 to 1 January 2028, methane-intensity reporting from August 2028 to August 2029, and intensity limits from August 2030 to August 2031. The Council and Parliament still need to reach agreement; this is not a final enacted postponement at the checked date.
- Sonatrach announced on 4 November 2026 an MoU with VNG AG and ICA Finance for emissions-reduction, low-carbon development and MRV systems. The announcement refers to earlier cooperation, including a June 2026 VNG agreement to cap methane emissions across the gas value chain.
- Sonatrach and Eni signed a protocol of intent on 17 August 2026 for GHG/methane reduction, technical analysis, capacity-building and site visits.

### OW-08 — EUDR plot-geolocation and supplier-evidence preparation in origin countries

**ESTABLISHED**

## 12. Updated cross-wave conclusions

1. **Regulatory transitions are lead generators, not businesses by themselves.** The buyer may have an internal compliance team, an incumbent adviser, a responsible importer or a regulated verifier already in place.
2. **Algerian access varies radically by market.** Cement exports have measurable EU trade exposure; gas exports have material energy-chain exposure but buyer concentration and established institutional partnerships; cocoa/coffee/wood traceability requires local origin-country presence; packaging and medical-device work needs a specific product/customer route.
3. **Subcontracting may be more plausible than direct product entry** in industrial markets, but only where a real procurement lot, supplier-qualification route, acceptance criteria, and payment terms are verified.
4. **No new candidate passed the hard entry gates.** CBAM data preparation remains research-only; industrial energy support remains partner-dependent; battery passport, EUDR and EUDAMED are deferred; methane is deferred for direct entry; PPWR remains open only for a product-specific material/composition problem.
5. **No software or physical product is justified to build yet.** The next deliverable should be evidence acquisition through a named buyer and bounded problem, not a platform.
