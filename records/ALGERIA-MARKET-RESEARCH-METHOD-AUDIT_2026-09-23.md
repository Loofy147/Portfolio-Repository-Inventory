# Algeria Market Research Method Audit — 2026-09-23

Status: OPEN / active methodological audit

Purpose:
Re-audit the full market-entry research method so the latest concepts do not become new blinders. This document supersedes any interpretation that treats "transition novelty" or "cross-boundary" as mandatory opportunity properties.

## 1. Core correction

The research anchor is not:
- sector;
- technology;
- novelty;
- cross-boundary alone;
- transaction alone;
- artifact alone.

The anchor is:

**Observable economic problem/event → consequence → current handling → gap/exception → affected owner → buying authority/payer → bounded intervention → payment test.**

A candidate may be:
- pre-transaction;
- during transaction;
- post-transaction;
- operational;
- incident-driven;
- compliance/readiness-driven;
provided the economic consequence and buyer are real.

## 2. What remains from the previous method

Keep:
- discovery + pre-kill in parallel;
- hard payer gate;
- actual buyer/PO authority distinction;
- regulatory-role separation;
- explicit acceptance path;
- bounded artifact/service;
- paid transaction test before dedicated product build;
- reuse existing technical primitives only after a paid case exposes a repeated primitive;
- evidence/status discipline;
- negative rules to prevent reopening killed ideas without changed assumptions.

## 3. What is demoted from "anchor" to "feature"

### Cross-boundary
Useful signal, not requirement.

It is often interesting because ownership becomes ambiguous, but an intra-team problem can still be commercially strong.

### Transaction
Useful unit of observation, not requirement.

Operational loss, readiness, prevention, maintenance decision, compliance preparation, and other pre/post-event work remain admissible.

### Novelty / Transition Delta
Useful freshness/change variable, not requirement.

Record:
- what changed;
- when;
- whether the organization has absorbed it;
but a recent change is never evidence of demand by itself.

### Evidence/artifact
Required for a bounded delivery model, but not necessarily the first discovery object.

### Regulatory recognition
Required only when the claimed value depends on a regulated/contractual acceptance role. Otherwise ordinary customer acceptance may suffice.

4. Urgency / deadline.

18. Budget owner.
19. Buyer/PO authority.
20. Actual payer.
21. Payment mechanism.
22. Payment timing.

E. Delivery

26. Acceptance criterion.
27. Delivery time.

29. Data/access required.

F. Entry

## 5. Hard gates

A candidate is not an entry candidate unless all are satisfied:

1. Real problem/event is evidenced.
2. Economic consequence is material enough to matter.
3. A specific legal entity can be identified as potential payer.
4. Buying authority is plausible and distinct from beneficiary where necessary.
5. Current workaround and existing market are understood.
6. No false regulatory/authority claim is needed.
7. Intervention is bounded.
8. First delivery does not require speculative product construction.
9. First transaction does not require meaningful working capital unless already available.
10. Acceptance and payment can occur directly enough to avoid dependence on an unproven third party.
11. Delivery economics plausibly support the transaction.
12. A paid test is possible.

## 6. Kill gates

Kill or defer when:

- problem is merely interesting but economically weak;
- problem is routine and already internalized adequately;
- incumbent suppliers already solve it with little visible gap;
- the buyer cannot be identified;
- payer is inferred only from beneficiary;
- purchase depends on someone else later getting paid;
- the service requires regulated authority we do not hold;
- working capital is material and unavailable;
- sales cycle is structurally long relative to ticket size;
- artifact has no objective acceptance criterion;
- data/access cannot be obtained legally and practically;
- liability is disproportionate to price;
- only the implementation is novel while the market problem is unchanged;
- a free/manual workaround is clearly sufficient.

## 7. Discovery principle

Search for **failure economics**, not "ideas".

Useful observation classes:

- rejection/refusal;
- delay/queue;
- rework;
- mismatch;
- dispute;
- evidence gap;
- hidden loss;
- exception;
- handoff failure;
- deadline pressure;
- new requirement;
- supplier/customer incompatibility;
- decision made with poor information;
- asset underperformance;
- irreversible or expensive error.

Then ask:
**Who loses? Who can buy? Why now?**

## 8. Opportunity vs entry distinction

Two separate judgments must never be conflated:

### Market opportunity
Does a real economic problem exist and can money plausibly be spent to solve it?

### Entry feasibility
Can this specific founder capture the first transaction with:
- low capital;
- current capabilities;
- manageable liability;
- reachable buyer;
- short enough sales cycle;
- direct payment?

A large market opportunity may still be a poor entry path.

## 9. Preferred first-transaction shape

Preferred:
**Observed case → direct buyer → fixed/bounded scope → short delivery → objective acceptance → direct invoice/payment → post-mortem**

Avoid:
**build product → hope market exists → acquire users → wait for network effects**

And avoid:
**customer → us → wait for government/insurer/client to approve/pay → cash**

## 10. Role of software/AI

Software and AI are implementation accelerators, not opportunity evidence.

Do not ask:
"What can AI automate?"

Ask:
"What economic failure can be detected/reduced/resolved cheaply, and would the payer buy that outcome?"

Existing reusable primitive:
Document → Structured Data → Decision → Evidence.

It is a capability reservoir, not a reason to force every opportunity into document automation.

## 11. Current strategic hypothesis

The strongest current hypothesis is broader than "cross-boundary exception":

**Under-served economic value often appears where an organization must move from one state to another and the existing handling is incomplete, costly, ambiguous, slow, or poorly evidenced.**

Cross-boundary cases are one important subclass because ownership frequently fragments.

Status:
HYPOTHESIS / OPEN.

## 12. Research loop

**Observe → Identify economic consequence → Map current handling → Pre-kill incumbents/internalization/regulation → Identify buyer/payer → Define minimal paid intervention → Run paid test → Measure delivery economics → Revalidate → Only then build/reuse software.**

## 13. Required evidence record for a paid experiment

Record:
- customer legal entity;
- buyer name/role;
- trigger date;
- actual problem;
- current workaround;
- value/loss;
- agreed scope;
- price quoted;
- price paid;
- payment date;
- delivery time;
- acceptance criterion;
- acceptance result;
- customer objection;
- whether work repeats;
- what failed;
- what remains UNKNOWN.

## 14. Research anti-bias rules

Do not:
- force all problems into cross-boundary framing;
- force all recent changes into opportunity framing;
- force all problems into AI/software;
- treat regulation as demand;
- treat job postings as willingness-to-pay;
- treat vendor presence as proof of unmet demand;
- treat absence of vendors online as proof of a gap;
- mistake beneficiary for payer;
- mistake a technically solvable problem for a commercially purchasable one;
- let an existing technical capability dictate the market search.

## 16. Immediate research implication

The next search should sample across:
- intra-function problems;
- cross-function problems;
- inter-company problems;
- regulated transitions;
- operational hidden losses;
- exception events;
- pre-transaction readiness;
- post-transaction acceptance/payment.

For each, run the same payer and entry-feasibility gates.

The objective is not to confirm the "cross-boundary" thesis. It is to find where the evidence survives after all plausible alternatives are killed.
