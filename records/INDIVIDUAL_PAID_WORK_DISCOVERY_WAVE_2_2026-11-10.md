# Individual Paid-Work Discovery — Wave 2

**Checked:** 2026-11-10  
**Operating unit:** Individual resident in Algeria; no startup/company assumed  
**Scope:** Paid work, bounties, technical task marketplaces, AI evaluation/data work, remote contracts and payout feasibility  
**State:** Discovery and screening only. No application, account creation, report submission, outreach or contract was made.

## 1. Executive result

This wave found **three individual-work channels worth a controlled eligibility check**, one conditional security-bounty channel, and several important payout exclusions.

1. **OpenTrain technical AI-training work** — multiple listings posted 9–10 November 2026 explicitly include Algeria, with advertised compensation from USD 20/hour to USD 120/hour equivalent. Several are paid per accepted task rather than hourly. This is the strongest current technical-task lead, but the contracting entity, whether each listing is first-party or external, and Algeria payout mechanics are not established. Current terms name WMD Global, LLC, state that Stripe/Stripe Connect or another processor may be used, and disclaim guaranteed hours or income. A current Privacy Policy page names OpenTrain AI, Inc.; this legal-entity mismatch needs clarification before submitting identity documents.
2. **Jwuma by Corpshore AI — Algeria language/data work** — an Algeria-specific page advertises Arabic, Tamazight and French annotation, translation review, model evaluation and QA work. It says payouts are weekly through Wise in USD by SWIFT to a USD-denominated bank account; ordinary local-currency accounts cannot receive this route. Live task volume and actual rates for Algeria are unknown. Its default earnings calculator is explicitly illustrative, not a rate offer.
3. **Bugcrowd researcher programmes** — official documentation supports bank transfer and PayPal, and provides a non-US individual tax form path. It also says the bank-account country field is populated based on the researcher's selected country. However, the public documentation retrieved does not explicitly establish that Algeria is currently supported by its payout processor. This is a **verify-before-reporting** channel, not a confirmed payout route.
4. **Upwork** — the platform has worldwide technical listings, but its Direct to Local Bank country list does **not** include Algeria. The USD wire option costs USD 50 per withdrawal, before possible bank/intermediary charges. Payoneer or PayPal may appear as alternatives, but actual availability and a compliant route to an Algerian bank must be confirmed in the individual account. This makes it a weak first route for small tickets unless a cheaper verified rail exists.

**Negative finding:** Prolific's participant country list excludes Algeria and directs unsupported countries to a waitlist. A researcher-side page mentioning small populations in Algeria does not override the participant eligibility rule.

**Legal boundary:** crypto-denominated bounty or grant payouts (for example, BTC/USDC) are not treated as an available Algeria-resident cash route. Law 25-10 adds article 6 bis to Law 05-01 and prohibits issuing, buying, selling, using, holding, trading or promoting virtual assets; the Bank of Algeria also lists Guideline 06/2025 on identifying, blocking and prohibiting operations related to virtual assets. Any crypto-denominated reward is therefore excluded from the actionable stack unless qualified Algerian counsel confirms the exact activity and payment treatment. This is not a reason to abandon security work; seek cash/fiat bank-transfer programmes instead.

No candidate in this wave is promoted to PAID-TEST-READY. The current best next step is to verify payout and contract terms **before** providing sensitive identity documents or performing unpaid qualification work.

### 3.2 Terms and counterparty checks

The OpenTrain Terms of Service search result updated 7 November 2026 identifies OpenTrain AI as a trade name of **WMD Global, LLC**. The current terms:
- classify talent as independent professionals/contractors, not employees;
- do not guarantee selection, work volume, hours or income;
- say payments are handled through payment processors and that Stripe (including Stripe Connect, where applicable) is currently used, with other processors possible;
- allow delayed/held/reversed payouts for compliance, disputes or risk reasons;
- state that clients own project work product under the default service contract, subject to the terms;
- say some listings are aggregated from external sources; for those, OpenTrain does not hold the contract or process the payment, and the external platform's terms govern.

**Identity discrepancy to resolve:** the OpenTrain Privacy Policy page retrieved separately identifies **OpenTrain AI, Inc.** and shows an earlier update date. The 7 November Terms identify WMD Global, LLC. This may reflect an entity/website update, but the discrepancy is unresolved. Confirm the data controller, contract party and recipient of application data before uploading identity documents.

**Payout feasibility:** Stripe's current Global Payouts availability page lists Algeria with DZD via SWIFT wire as Preview. That is a possible infrastructure path for a platform able and authorised to use Global Payouts. It does **not** prove OpenTrain has enabled this product for Algeria. Stripe's ordinary account availability list is different from Global Payouts recipient-country coverage.

### 3.3 Required written questions before sensitive onboarding

1. Is the exact listing a direct OpenTrain project or an external/referral listing?
2. Which legal entity signs the independent-contractor agreement with an Algeria-resident individual?
3. Which processor and product pays an Algeria-based contractor: Stripe Connect, Stripe Global Payouts, or another named provider?
4. Is Algeria enabled for this exact project, in the live payout configuration—not merely in the job's candidate-country list?
5. Is payment to an individual's Algerian bank account by DZD SWIFT supported? What are the minimum, fees, expected timing, currency conversion and returned-payment rules?
6. What is the acceptance rubric, rework window, average actual paid task duration, payment schedule and minimum task volume?
7. What work-product/IP assignment applies, and what background tools/code remain the contributor's property?
8. Which party receives the CV, phone number, LinkedIn profile, interview recording/transcript and identity documents, and for how long?

Do not treat the displayed hourly equivalent as earnings until a sample task's actual completion time, acceptance/rejection rate and payment are known.

## 6. Upwork — work supply exists; low-cost payout does not

Upwork's Direct to Local Bank documentation lists supported countries and currencies; **Algeria is not on the current list**. Where enabled, that route costs USD 0.99 and generally arrives within four business days. The absence of Algeria matters more than the general headline that Direct to Local Bank is available internationally.

Upwork's USD wire transfer charges USD 50 per withdrawal; the receiving/intermediary bank may add fees and the transfer may take up to seven business days. PayPal and Payoneer are offered as additional payout providers in some countries, but the exact options shown for an Algeria-based profile and the ability to withdraw to a compliant local bank account must be tested in the account.

A current example of worldwide technical work was a Python backend engineer contract (Python/FastAPI/Django/Flask, PostgreSQL, Linux, APIs; USD 10–30/hour, 1–3 months; listing showed 20–50 proposals and an established client with prior spend). This is evidence that worldwide technical demand exists, not a recommendation to apply or evidence of this individual's eligibility/fit.
- Example: https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Python-backend-engineer_~022108694761033224254/

**Decision:** keep Upwork as a secondary sourcing channel, not the default small-ticket payout path. Do not apply to low-value fixed-price work if the only usable transfer method costs USD 50. Verify the actual Payoneer/PayPal options and a bank-confirmed withdrawal path first.

### 7.2 Crypto-denominated rewards

Law 25-10 of 24 July 2025 added article 6 bis to Law 05-01. Its text prohibits issuing, buying, selling, using, holding, trading or promoting virtual assets, whether as a means of payment/currency or as an investment instrument; mining is also included. Bank of Algeria Guideline 06/2025 is listed as relating to identifying, blocking and prohibiting operations associated with virtual assets.

This creates a direct legal boundary for bounty programmes that pay in BTC, ETH, USDC or other cryptoassets. The opportunity may be technically open on the platform but is not a lawful individual cash route to assume for an Algeria resident. This applies to crypto payouts from otherwise genuine bug-bounty or open-source programmes, not just trading platforms.

Decision: **KILL crypto payout route for the Algeria-resident operating unit pending written legal clarification.** Do not use a third-party wallet, another person's account, false residence, conversion intermediary or misdescription of a payment as a workaround. Continue searching for cash bank transfer, lawful service-export payment and grant disbursement routes.

Official/regulatory references:
- Bank of Algeria Guideline 06/2025 listing: https://www.bank-of-algeria.dz/ar/المبادئ-التوجيهية/
- Bank of Algeria French guidelines listing: https://www.bank-of-algeria.dz/les-lignes-directrice/
- Official legal text mirror sourced to the Ministry of Justice: https://www.algerialawlibrary.com/laws/loi-n-25-10-du-28-moharram-1447-correspondant-au-24-juillet-?lang=fr
