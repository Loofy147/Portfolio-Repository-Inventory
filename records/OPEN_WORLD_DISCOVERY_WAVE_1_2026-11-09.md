# Open-World Economic Discovery — Wave 1 — 2026-11-09

**Scope:** cross-sector, cross-geography first wave. No existing project or repository was used as the discovery boundary.  
**Method:** observable economic event/problem → consequence → current handling → pre-kill → buyer/payer → bounded intervention → acceptance/payment test.  
**Coverage:** Algeria financial services, trade/public procurement, border conformity infrastructure and digital-trust regulation; EU packaging and carbon-border rules; current EU procurement serving Algeria. This is a broad sample, not an exhaustive global census.

## Executive decision

**No new product or service is promoted to TEST-READY or PURSUE.** One live public procurement is verified, but the user's direct entry eligibility is not established. One new market hypothesis—factoring-specific receivables evidence readiness—survives initial screening but lacks a named paying customer and demonstrated externalized need. The other examined regulatory/service wedges are deferred or killed at the generic-entry level.

The useful result is not a new product idea. It is a short list of real changes, one live procurement lead, and explicit reasons not to build prematurely.

## 2. Strongest new market hypothesis: factoring-specific receivables evidence readiness

**Candidate ID:** `DZ-FAC-2026-001`  
**Evidence status:** ESTABLISHED regulatory change + LOCAL_MARKET_SIGNAL; commercial wedge remains HYPOTHESIS  
**Lifecycle:** DISCOVERED / SCREENED — not TEST-READY

### Observed change

Algeria's Bank of Algeria introduced a formal factoring framework through Regulation 26-03 dated 2026-08-13, published in Official Gazette No. 67 in September 2026. Instruction 09-2026 dated 2026-10-07 specifies practical operating steps; APS reported the instruction on 2026-10-22.

The framework:
- reserves habitual factoring activity to licensed banks and financial institutions;
- requires the factor to assess the applicant/assignor and debtor;
- limits eligible claims to commercial receivables meeting specified conditions, including certainty, liquidity, determinable amount, no debtor dispute, a payment term under six months from invoice, transferability and no disqualifying prior assignment/security or debtor insolvency condition;
- requires invoices or equivalent documents and a summary bordereau;
- formalizes subrogation through a receipt, visible invoice notation and written debtor notification;
- requires reporting of factoring operations to the enterprise/household risk centre.

The factor, not a new software startup, is the regulated financing provider.

### Economic problem and potential intervention

A supplier may have delivered goods/services and issued a valid invoice but still have cash tied up until the debtor pays. Factoring can convert an eligible receivable into earlier liquidity. The new workflow introduces eligibility checks, debtor information, supporting evidence, transfer documents and notification steps.

**Potential bounded service hypothesis:** a one-time, non-financial-advice receivables evidence pack for an SME seeking factoring:
- reconcile invoice to delivery/acceptance evidence;
- identify partial payments, credit notes, disputes and due dates;
- organize debtor identity and available financial evidence;
- check documentary completeness against a bank's published intake checklist;
- produce a traceable file and exception list.

This is not factoring, lending, underwriting, guarantee, debt collection or a promise of bank acceptance. Any service boundary requires Algerian legal review before sale.

### Buyer/payer and alternatives

- **Possible payer 1:** SME/assignor seeking to prepare a factoring application.
- **Possible payer 2:** bank/financial institution seeking a workflow or document-quality solution.
- **Possible payer 3:** insurance partner supporting the factoring process.
- **Observed market signal:** CAGEX and BEA reportedly signed an insurance contract related to factoring on 2026-10-29. This confirms institutional activity around the new mechanism, not actual factoring volume, service outsourcing or a software procurement.
- **Alternatives:** SME finance/accounting staff, its accountant, the bank's own relationship and credit teams, existing ERP/accounting tools and internal onboarding.
- **Existing kill constraint:** prior market research already killed generic invoicing/collection outsourcing. This candidate is not allowed to reopen that generic service. It survives only if the new factoring-specific eligibility/evidence workflow creates a distinct, externally purchased gap.

### Hard-gate result

- Real regulatory process: ESTABLISHED.
- Material SME working-capital problem: supported as a general market issue, but no current customer case was observed in this wave.
- Named buyer/payer: possible classes identified, no specific paying legal entity.
- Purchase authority: UNKNOWN.
- Current workflow/outsourcing gap: UNKNOWN.
- Bounded first delivery without software: plausible.
- Legal scope: OPEN; must avoid regulated financial activity/advice.
- Working capital: low for a document-preparation service, but confidentiality, accuracy and liability still matter.
- Objective acceptance: could be completeness against a named factor's intake requirements, not financing approval.
- Paid test: none.
- Time-to-cash/unit economics: UNKNOWN.

**Decision:** retain as a narrow hypothesis only. No platform, CRM, underwriting engine, invoice registry or factoring marketplace. Do not claim there is no invoice registry or that banks lack controls; those claims were not established by authoritative evidence.

**Next discriminating test:** obtain a real anonymized rejected/returned factoring file or a bank-published checklist; establish which missing evidence caused rework; identify one SME with eligible receivables and a named factor; verify whether the SME or factor would pay for a fixed-scope preparation service. If the bank handles this internally or the SME will not pay, kill the service wedge.

### 3.1 Generic CBAM compliance service for Algerian exporters

**Candidate ID:** `EU-CBAM-MAGHREB-2026-001`  
**Status:** DEFERRED / generic entry wedge KILLED

- The EU definitive CBAM regime began 2026-01-01.
- The EU 50-tonne threshold exempts smaller importers; importers above the threshold for covered goods must follow the applicable authorization and reporting regime.
- Importers can use Commission default values or actual values. To use actual values, the third-country producer must supply verified embedded-emissions data.
- The EU Commission published verifier/accreditation guidance on 2026-08-24 and registry access guidance in September 2026.
- SGS Algeria markets CBAM pre-verification and technical support.
- virESG markets a Maghreb-focused CBAM platform and explicitly advertises Algeria/Sonelgaz integration.

**Pre-kill:** generic reporting software, carbon accounting and pre-verification are already served by established certification and specialist providers. Verification/accreditation is a specialist boundary. No named Algerian exporter with an unmet gap or paid request was identified.

**Decision:** do not build another CBAM platform or claim an underserved Algerian market. Reopen only for a named installation/exporter with a measurable supplier-data gap that incumbents do not solve and a direct payer.

### 3.3 Algerian electronic trust-service provider

**Candidate ID:** `DZ-TRUST-2026-001`  
**Status:** DEFERRED / direct provider entry KILLED for this founder context

Law 26-02 dated 2026-02-17 (Official Gazette No. 14, 2026-02-18) establishes a national authority and a wider trust-service framework. Economic trust-service provision is subject to a service-specific licence. The law requires an eligible legal person under Algerian law (or Algerian nationality for a natural person), sufficient financial capacity and relevant ICT expertise; it also provides for qualification, evaluation audit, insurance, data-localization requirements and penalties for unlicensed provision. Transitional provisions keep existing authorities operating until the new authority is effectively installed and implementing texts are issued.

**Pre-kill:** this is not a low-capital software-product opening. Provider status is regulated and capital/assurance intensive. No current procurement or named paying customer for an adjacent integration service was identified.

**Decision:** do not enter as a trust-service provider. An integration/security support role for an already authorized provider remains a hypothesis only if that provider names a paid work package.

Source: Official Gazette text hosted by the Ministry of Post and Telecommunications: https://www.mpt.gov.dz/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Loi-n%C2%B0-26-02.Services-de-Confiance.Identification-electronique.AR_.pdf
